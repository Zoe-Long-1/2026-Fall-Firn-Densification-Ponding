About the model

A one-dimensional column of firn (compacting snow on an ice sheet) is forced by a seasonal air temperature. The model solves the heat equation

ρ c ∂T/∂t = ∂/∂z ( k(ρ) ∂T/∂z ) + L R

with the Crank–Nicolson finite-volume method on layers that move down as new snow is buried. The surface temperature is the air temperature capped at 0 °C (or 0 °C while a pond exists); the base has zero heat flux. Coupled to the heat equation:

  • Densification (Herron and Langway, 1980): each layer compacts at a rate that increases with temperature and accumulation.
  • Melt: a degree-day model melts the surface when the air is above 0 °C.
  • Percolation and refreezing: meltwater moves down layer by layer, refreezing where the firn is cold. Refreezing releases latent heat (the L R term) and adds ice.
  • Perched water and ponds: layers denser than the impermeable density block water, which fills the firn above them. When that water reaches the surface, a pond forms and drains at the runoff rate.

Python runs in your browser through Pyodide; nothing is sent to a server. The verification notebook in the repository compares the heat solver with the analytical solution for a seasonal temperature wave.
